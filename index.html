Homelessness & Poverty in India

Text-Only Awareness and Educational App

Introduction

Homelessness and extreme poverty remain serious challenges in India. Millions of people live without permanent shelter, stable income, clean water, sanitation, or access to healthcare.

Who Are the Homeless?

Homeless people in India include daily-wage laborers, migrant workers, elderly individuals, women, children, and people with disabilities. Many work long hours but still cannot afford housing.

Major Statistics (Estimates)

  • India has an estimated 1.7 to 2 million homeless people.
  • Over 65 million people live in slums or temporary housing.
  • Nearly 30–40% of the urban population lives in informal settlements.
  • About 1 in 5 urban workers earns less than the basic cost of living.
  • More than 50% of homeless adults depend on daily-wage labor.
  • Homeless people are 3 to 4 times more likely to suffer from untreated illness.
  • Millions of children remain out of school due to poverty and housing insecurity.
  • Life expectancy among the homeless is significantly lower than the national average.
  • Women and children form a large and vulnerable share of the homeless population.

Daily Life Struggles

Homeless individuals often sleep on footpaths, railway stations, bus stops, under bridges, or in open spaces. They face hunger, unsafe drinking water, extreme weather, and constant fear of eviction.

Impact on Children

Children without stable housing often miss school, suffer from malnutrition, and are forced into child labor. Without education, they are likely to remain trapped in poverty as adults.

Health Consequences

Poor sanitation and lack of medical access lead to high rates of infections, respiratory diseases, malnutrition, and mental health issues. Small illnesses often become life-threatening.

Why We Must Fix This

  • Homelessness weakens the national economy.
  • Unhealthy populations increase healthcare costs.
  • Uneducated children reduce future productivity.
  • Poverty increases crime, exploitation, and insecurity.
  • Providing housing improves public health and safety.
  • Every human deserves dignity, shelter, and opportunity.

What Can Be Done

  • Increase affordable housing projects.
  • Improve access to education and skill training.
  • Expand shelters and healthcare services.
  • Support NGOs and community programs.
  • Create stable employment opportunities.

Conclusion

Homelessness is not just a personal failure but a social issue. With awareness, policy action, and compassion, India can reduce poverty and provide a better future for millions.
